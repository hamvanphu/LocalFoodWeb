Audience: 200-300 Project Managers in FPT Automotive
Goal: Build a generation of AI-Native Project Managers, not simply PMs who can use ChatGPT.

0. One-minute summary

In one line: move PMs from work management to PM + AI orchestration so they can direct AI to build, operate, and govern real delivery systems.

What participants learn

Component Timing Core outcome
Kick-off 1 hour (week 1) Understand CASAN (comparable to Gartner's 5-level AI Maturity Model) maturity, the L0-L5 delegation ladder, leash A/A+, and why real human hours matter more than token counts.
Exercises EX-01 to EX-06 Weeks 1-3 (self-paced) Complete 6 exercises across the SDLC: requirements, WBS, estimation, risk, reports, dashboards, delegation, and governance. Work independently with AI coding agent as coach.
Capstone - Build your Customer Zero (= dogfooding) Weeks 4-5 Direct AI to build a working vertical slice, keep a Dev Book (= Decision Log / ADR), collect telemetry, and defend the result in viva. No working system + no viva pass = no graduation.
Clinic 2 hours/week throughout Volunteer to present your work and learn from peers. The clinic is the presentation and learning session.

What participants bring

  • One real project to apply the training to, or one of the six sample project briefs.
  • An AI coding agent account (e.g. Claude Code) and a safe environment for project materials. The AI agent serves as the "super orchestrator" used throughout every step -- see the Orchestrator Guide.
  • Five weeks of blended effort on top of their real delivery work.
  • Willingness to work hands-on, even if they are not coders today. No prior coding knowledge required.

What participants leave with

  • Capability: operate at CASAN Level 2 (Augmented), with a selected core moving toward Levels 3-4. Able to direct AI to build + operate + govern + measure ROI, and not rubber-stamp.
  • Deliverables: a working Customer Zero system slice, a Dev Book (AI-wrong / PM-corrected), real telemetry, a before/after case study, an AI Workflow + Delegation Map, and an operational dashboard.
  • Certification: AI-Native PM -- Certified / Distinction (see Evaluation and certification).

Three hard graduation gates:
1. Pass the six capability pillars.
2. Demonstrate a working system live.
3. Pass the viva defense.
Missing one gate means no graduation.

1. Program positioning

The program does not train:

  • PMs who only know how to ask better chatbot questions.
  • Prompt engineering in isolation.
  • Old workflows with AI pasted on top.

The program does train PMs who can:

  • Run projects in an AI-first operating model.
  • Lead a mixed human + AI workforce.
  • Apply governance: human-in-the-loop, approval, audit, and safe delegation.
  • Measure productivity and ROI with telemetry instead of gut feel.
  • Avoid the most dangerous failure mode: rubber-stamping AI output.

2. Core philosophy

2.1 Human-led, AI-first, Outcome-first

  • Human-led: people decide, carry judgment, and own the result.
  • AI-first: AI drafts or executes first; humans review and steer the next step.
  • Outcome-first: delivery speed, quality, cost, and learning matter more than tool activity.

2.2 The deployable unit is (PM + AI)

The program treats the PM and AI as a single operating pair:

  • AI provides breadth, speed, and production force.
  • The PM provides depth, judgment, risk control, and accountability.
  • The goal is not to turn PMs into developers; it is to help PMs direct AI-native execution responsibly.

3. Capability model

3.1 CASAN maturity

Level Name Meaning
1 Curious Ad hoc AI use, no workflow, no measurement
2 Augmented AI strengthens the PM workflow with basic telemetry
3 Standard AI-first delivery is standardized team-wide
4 Automated Agents run bounded workflows with gates and audit
5 Native AI-native delivery operates naturally at scale

Target: move the broad cohort to Level 2, while building a core group that can reach Levels 3-4.

3.2 Delegation ladder

Level Name AI is allowed to...
L0 Observe Summarize or observe without changing anything
L1 Draft Produce drafts for full human review
L2 Recommend Propose options and analysis
L3 Execute (bounded) Execute low-risk steps within tight boundaries
L4 Operate workflow Run a bounded workflow with gates and exception handling
L5 Restricted High-risk autonomy; intentionally not granted here

3.3 Six capability pillars

Pillar What “meets the bar” means
AI Literacy Understand enough about LLMs, context, tools, and agents to use AI safely and effectively
AI Delegation Distinguish what AI should do, what humans should do, and where review gates belong
Workflow Design Redesign SDLC work into AI-first steps with clear checkpoints
Governance & Risk Control hallucination, security, IP, approval, and auditability
Telemetry & Economics Read dashboards, understand cost, and reason about ROI
Outcome Leadership Lead the human + AI system toward delivery, quality, and margin outcomes

4. Program structure (5 weeks)

Kick-off (1 hour, week 1)

A single one-hour session focused on:

  • Course overview and the future of software engineering and PM work.
  • The shift from human-first to AI-first execution.
  • CASAN self-positioning for both the PM and the project.
  • Receive project brief and commit to "Customer Zero".

After the kick-off, members work independently with their AI coding agent as coach.

Exercises (weeks 1-3, self-paced)

Six exercises (EX-01 to EX-06) covering the SDLC:

  • EX-01 Requirements (write and review)
  • EX-02 WBS
  • EX-03 Estimation (3-point, with mandatory trade-off)
  • EX-04 Risk Register
  • EX-05 Weekly Report + Dashboard (catch AI fabricating numbers)
  • EX-06 Delegation Map + Leash

Members work at their own pace. Every week: 2-hour clinic -- volunteer to present work and learn from peers.

Capstone (weeks 4-5)

Participants must build a working vertical slice and operate it as their own Customer Zero:

  • A real end-to-end flow.
  • A Dev Book capturing AI mistakes and human corrections.
  • Real telemetry.
  • A final viva defense.

5. Hands-on assignments

The program uses ten assignments on real project work:

# Assignment Key requirement
T1 AI-written requirements Includes stakeholder negotiation simulation (stakeholder demands scope additions)
T2 AI review of requirements Identify contradictions, gaps, and ambiguity; PM classifies real vs false findings
T3 AI-generated WBS Breakdown with levels and dependencies; PM adjusts
T4 AI-based estimation 3-point estimation with trade-off iteration (re-scope when estimate exceeds target)
T5 AI-created risk register Likelihood x impact + mitigation for 10+ risks
T6 AI-assisted test strategy Coverage, test levels, quality risks
T7 AI-generated weekly report Includes hallucination detection step (verify AI-generated figures against source data)
T8 AI-generated meeting summary Action items with owners and deadlines
T9 AI-assisted retrospective Telemetry-based insights, not gut feel
T10 Minimum viable delivery dashboard 5+ KPIs with formulas and data sources

Every submission includes:

  • the artifact,
  • the prompt pattern or instruction used,
  • telemetry,
  • evidence of human review.

Passing the exercises

  • All 6 exercises must score 2 or higher.
  • Risk and dashboard work are mandatory.
  • Missing telemetry means the assignment is incomplete.

6. Governance principles

The program turns governance from slogans into mechanisms:

Mechanism What it means
Fail-closed gate If the output cannot be verified, it is blocked by default
Built-flagged, awaiting approval AI may complete work, but high-risk release still requires explicit human approval
Hard-stop on conflict If the spec is contradictory or ambiguous, AI must stop and surface the issue instead of guessing

Non-negotiable rules

  • AI does not self-merge or self-release.
  • AI does not touch secrets or restricted materials.
  • AI does not mark work as done while blocked by review or hard-stop conditions.

7. AI agent as the super orchestrator

The training uses an AI coding agent (e.g. Claude Code) as a super orchestrator, not just a chat interface. The PM directs the agent with goals, not keystrokes.

Standard operating loop:

Context -> Plan -> Delegate (Leash A/A+) -> Execute -> Gate (fail-closed) -> Log (Dev Book + telemetry) -> Repeat

This matters because:

  • one goal replaces ten micro-actions,
  • the PM can stay focused on judgment instead of keystrokes,
  • autonomy is always paired with explicit gates (Leash A/A+ as the guardrail),
  • the Dev Book and telemetry become built-in evidence.

Introductory guide for participants: Orchestrator Guide -- 7 steps, 8 "control levers", quickstart, prompt patterns, and a checklist against rubber-stamping. Introduced at the kick-off, used throughout the entire 5 weeks.

8. Capstone and Customer Zero

The capstone is the heart of the program.

Participants must show:

  1. A working system that runs live (a vertical slice, not a full application).
  2. A Dev Book that clearly records where AI was wrong and how the PM corrected it.
  3. Real telemetry including human hours, overrides, and token reconciliation.
  4. An operating log describing what broke when the PM actually used the system.

Why this matters

Without this requirement, the program could accidentally reward polished AI-generated documents without real understanding. The Customer Zero model forces participants to build, run, observe, and correct.

Two-tier Capstone

Tier For whom What to deliver
Tier 1 -- Full-stack PMs with a technical background Real running code -- a working vertical slice deployed locally or on a server
Tier 2 -- PM-stack Non-coder PMs Prototype + API mock + SQL schema + test scenarios -- demonstrating the full build-operate-review loop without production code

Both tiers require a real Dev Book and qualify for Certified. Details in the Capstone Playbook.

10. KPI and telemetry

The program measures:

  • AI adoption
  • planning lead time
  • estimation accuracy
  • rework rate
  • review time
  • delivery quality
  • project margin
  • Nen / Compression (traditional hours / real hours) -- the leverage metric for leadership
  • token efficiency (MD delivered / 1M tokens consumed)

Measurement rules

  • Use real human hours, not token counts as a proxy.
  • Reconcile estimated token usage with actual usage.
  • Log work outside the original plan so the efficiency story stays honest.
  • Tie all numbers back to delivery outcomes and ROI.
